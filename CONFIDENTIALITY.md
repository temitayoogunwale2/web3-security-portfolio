# Confidentiality and Responsible Disclosure

## 1. Purpose

This document describes the principles I follow when handling audit evidence, security findings, client information, and public research.

## 2. Confidentiality First

I do not publish client-owned code, private audit reports, undisclosed vulnerabilities, credentials, private keys, seed phrases, personal information, or confidential communications without appropriate authorisation.

## 3. What May Be Published

Subject to engagement terms and disclosure permission, public material may include:

- General audit methodology and security checklists.
- Sanitised case studies that do not identify a confidential client or target.
- Controlled demonstrations using purpose-built example contracts.
- Aggregate experience statements that can be substantiated.
- Findings already publicly disclosed by the authorised owner, with appropriate attribution.

## 4. What Must Remain Private

I do not publish:

- Undisclosed or unpatched vulnerabilities without explicit authorisation.
- Audit reports or excerpts restricted by an NDA or programme rules.
- Client names, target addresses, or timelines where these reveal confidential engagement details.
- Credentials, private keys, seed phrases, production data, or private wallet material.
- Claims about severity, financial impact, client engagements, or rewards that cannot be substantiated.

## 5. Case Study Review

Before publishing a case study, I:

1. Review the engagement terms, NDA, and disclosure rules.
2. Obtain written permission where required.
3. Remove identifying information and inspect screenshots, logs, metadata, and commit history.
4. Prefer a purpose-built reproduction over publishing confidential target code.
5. Clearly describe redactions and evidence limitations.

## 6. Responsible Disclosure

Potential vulnerabilities are handled according to the applicable programme rules, owner contact process, authorisation, and disclosure timeline. Public disclosure is made only when permitted and appropriate.

## 7. When in Doubt

If I cannot establish that material is safe and authorised for publication, I keep it private and publish a generalised methodology note or controlled demonstration instead.

---

Protecting users, clients, and affected systems takes priority over publishing a finding.
